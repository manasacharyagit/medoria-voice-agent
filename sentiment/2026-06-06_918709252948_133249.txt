This suggests he was struggling through his non-preferred language during the most critical part of the call.

Aria deserves massive praise for her **multilingual flexibility**. The transition to Hindi was flawless, grammatically natural, and culturally polite. Furthermore, Aria's recovery after the user's "Bye" and subsequent "Is there anyone?" was handled beautifully with warmth and reassurance.

### 6. Actionable Recommendations for Medoria

* **Introduce Upfront Language Selection:** 
  If the system detects an Indian locale or name, or simply as a standard opening, Aria should offer a quick language choice: *"For English, press 1 or say English. Hindi ke liye 2 dabayein ya Hindi bolein."* Alternatively, Aria can detect the language of the user's query dynamically if they speak in Hindi first.
* **Redesign the Email Verification Flow:** 
  Instead of asking users to spell long, complex email addresses letter-by-letter (which is tedious and error-prone), Aria should read back what she *thinks* she heard and ask for confirmation: *"I think I heard manasacharya160@gmail.com, is that correct?"* If it's incorrect, only then ask for clarification, or offer to send a secure booking link via SMS to complete their profile.
* **Detect Email/Data Mismatches:** 
  Implement a guardrail where if a user verbally states one email ("160") but spells another ("1702"), the AI gently flags it: *"I noticed you spelled it with 1702, but earlier mentioned 160. Which one is correct?"* This ensures data integrity.
* **Active Re-engagement During Silence:** 
  When the user went silent and then asked *"Is there anyone?"*, it means there was a dead-air gap. Medoria should implement a gentle "re-engagement" prompt (e.g., a soft *"I'm still here, Mast! Take your time."* or a comfort chime) to let the caller know they haven't been abandoned.
